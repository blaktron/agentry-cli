# Choosing a look

A report design serves reading first. The person's readers open a report to read it, so the body stays calm and the brand shows on the cover, the header and the rules [src: web:https://docs.secureagentry.net/runner/].

## Start from the brief

Write down, before anything else, three words for the mood (sober, warm, technical, bold, institutional) and who reads the reports. A board pack, a field report and a studio proposal want different designs. When the brief names colours or faces, use them, shaded to pass contrast.

## Colours

- **From a logo.** Look at the logo: its strongest colour is usually the accent. A second colour in it is a candidate secondary. Darken a light brand colour until it passes: `check_design` gives the nearest shade.
- **From references.** Take the dominant colour and one support colour, never more. A reference's photography is not a colour scheme: look at its type and its blocks of colour.
- **From a page.** A site's text may name its brand colours or its fonts. A colour named in a stylesheet you cannot see is not one you know.
- **Two at most.** The accent does most of the work. A secondary earns its place on a split or geometric cover, or not at all.
- **Dark accents carry white text.** Navy, forest, burgundy and charcoal give a band or plate title real contrast. A bright accent (orange, yellow, light blue) colours rules and shapes and never text: choose a cover that keeps text near-black (`geometric`, `framed`, `split` with a dark secondary).

## Faces

Pair one family for headings with one for the body, and a code face. Pairings that work:

| Mood | Headings | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Institutional | Libre Baskerville | Source Serif 4 |
| Editorial | Playfair Display | Source Sans 3 |
| Warm, literary | Fraunces | Nunito Sans |
| Modern, plain | Inter | Inter |
| Technical | IBM Plex Sans | IBM Plex Sans |
| Bold, studio | Space Grotesk | DM Sans |
| Civic, classic | Crimson Pro | Crimson Pro |
| Elegant, light | Raleway | Raleway |

A display face (Playfair Display, Fraunces, Montserrat) is for headings only. Keep the body in a face made for long reading.

## Structure

- **Title block:** `band` is confident and needs a dark accent; `ruled` is quiet; `bar` sits between.
- **Headings:** `rule` for reports with many sections, `smallcaps` for formal ones, `numbered` for technical and legal documents.
- **Header and footer:** `brandLine` with `{brand}` left and `{date}` right for a firm; `titleAndPage` with `{title}` and `{page}` for long documents. Put `{confidentiality}` in the footer when the brief mentions confidential work.
- **Margins:** `wide` reads as premium and suits serif bodies; `narrow` suits dense technical reports.

## Cover style

Choose the built-in style that matches the mood: `split` for a firm with a brand to show, `largeType` for a bold single statement, `geometric` for a modern look with two colours, `framed` for formal reports, `band` for a safe default, `plate` when the design has a cover image. Set `when` to `always` when the person wants every titled report to open with a cover, `long` to keep short reports cover-free.
